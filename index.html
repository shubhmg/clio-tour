CLIOTHE ART IS EVERYDAY

Every screen of a purchasing ERP, end to end.

Clio is a quantity-only purchasing ERP for a laminates importer. It exists to answer one question every morning — what should we reorder, and when — by holding each SKU's runway against a 45–75 day lead time from China. There is no money anywhere in it: not a price, not a total, not a currency symbol.

A complete tour of the shipping product, captured from the live demo in Clio's glass design language. Switch theme and device above any screen.

The decision

Clio exists to answer one question every morning — what should we reorder, and when. These are the screens that answer it.

4 screens
01

Dashboard

The morning briefing: how many SKUs need ordering today, what capital is frozen, what is at sea, and whether sales are pacing.

  • Four counts lead — to order now, due this month, dead stock, on the water
  • Order-today panel ranks the six most urgent SKUs, each with a runway-vs-lead bar and its order-by date
  • Stock health states one sentence over a running/slow/dead composition bar
  • Lead-time pressure prints the thesis: you hold ~X days of stock but need Y days to reorder from China
02

Purchasing

The order desk. Decide, factory by factory, what goes on a container this week so nothing runs dry before the next one lands.

  • position = on hand + inbound; runout = position ÷ daily pace; order-by = runout − lead time
  • Honest buckets: now, soon, healthy — plus “No sales” and “Set lead time” rather than a fake number
  • Factory sections start collapsed; the headers are the summary you scan first
  • Ticked rows collect into an order basket
03

A SKU’s story

Why this SKU is on the list, in a sentence — then the arithmetic behind it.

  • “Stock lasts 25 days, a reorder takes 55 days to land — you’d be out for 30 days”
  • A timeline with the shortfall hatched and the landing date marked
  • A cover box with a quantity slider: what ordering N covers you to, and when the next order falls due
  • Inline fixes for the two things that block planning — pick the factory, set its lead time
04

Planning settings

The assumptions behind every order-by date, with the arithmetic stated rather than implied.

  • Reorder every 30/45/60/90 days — each order is sized to last that long after it lands
  • Pace: recent (last 60 days ÷ 60) or last year (the same 60 days a year ago), with the real date window shown
  • A min-stock floor beneath the pace model, for SKUs with no sales to judge by
  • Units or packs — the app-wide switch

The catalogue

Catalog → design group → SKU. The group is the hub: people reason about designs, not lone SKUs.

3 screens
05

Products

Find any SKU and see which design group and which factory it belongs to.

  • Items and Designs tabs, server-side search across name and SKU
  • Scope by catalog, group and factory — each with an explicit “No catalog / No factory” bucket
  • Rows expand to ledger, design, edit and delete
06

Catalogs

The physical sample folders that sit at the top of the hierarchy.

  • Each row carries live group and SKU counts
  • Focus sets the app-wide scope to that catalog and jumps to Purchasing
  • Deleting a catalog unlinks its groups rather than destroying them
07

Parties

The Chinese factories you buy from and the Indian customers you sell to.

  • Supplier / customer switch with infinite scroll
  • A supplier opens its products builder; a customer opens its ledger
  • The supplier record holds the lead time — the other half of every order-by date

Buying

A purchase order becomes a factory order, rides a container, and lands as stock. One chain, four screens.

8 screens
08

Purchase Orders

Factory orders from placed, through ready, to loaded into a container.

  • Grouped by order date, one row per factory slice
  • A ready bar appears the moment ordered, ready and shipped stop agreeing
  • Selecting ready orders raises a bar to consolidate them into one container
09

Everything for one order

Tapping a row opens every action for it in one place — no three-dot menu to hunt for.

  • The state change is the primary button: Mark ready, or Goods received
  • Edit the order, download the factory’s Excel, open the underlying voucher
  • Destructive actions sit last and read red
10

Raising an order

One purchase order means one supplier. Raised on exactly the screen a purchase is entered on.

  • Pick the supplier first, then search every SKU
  • Adding a product the supplier doesn’t make prompts rather than blocking it — a one-off or a new factory is legitimate
  • Quantities step in the SKU’s own pack, and store in base units
11

Containers

Every container sailing China → warehouse, grouped by when it lands.

  • The voyage rail draws itself on load, the ship riding its real position along the route
  • Colour only when it means something — amber arriving soon, brick overdue
  • Voyage time is a business-wide setting, editable in place
12

Receiving a container

Marking a container arrived is the moment goods become stock.

  • It adds the units into the destination warehouse and posts a purchase invoice per factory
  • Reopen pulls the stock back out and cancels those invoices
  • The same sheet carries the Excel export and the edit
13

Purchases

The register of goods actually received — quantity only, no money anywhere.

  • Date-grouped rows, each listing every line with its quantity and unit
  • Swipe a row to cancel, with a mandatory reason
  • Scope resolves against the line items, not just the header
14

Sales

Goods dispatched to customers — the outgoing movement that defines velocity.

  • These postings are the 60-day ledger that daily pace is computed from
  • Same row grammar as Purchases
  • Cancelling asks why
15

Entering a purchase

The entry screen shared by purchases, sales, transfers and orders.

  • Search adds a line; the bar keeps focus so you can rattle off a whole invoice
  • Quantities can be typed in units or in packs — storage is always base units
  • No rate, no tax, no total: Clio is quantity-only by design

Stock

What you hold, where it is, what is moving, and what has stopped.

5 screens
16

Stock Health

What is selling, what is over-stocked, and what is frozen — measured on sales.

  • Dead = no sale in 90 days · Slow = over 120 days of cover · Running = selling through
  • The three tiles are the filter; tapping one narrows the list and tapping it again clears
  • Counts recount from whatever scope is applied, so the tiles can never disagree with the list
17

Health by catalog

Health rolled up the way the catalogue is actually organised, worst first.

  • Collapsed by default — at 1,500 SKUs the rollup is the answer, the SKUs are the drill-in
  • Each header carries its own dead/slow counts and a mix bar
  • Opening a catalog reveals its design groups; picking one switches to the design view with that group open
18

Stock & Warehouses

The live position across warehouses, plus the warehouse master itself.

  • Drill catalog → design → SKU with share meters and low-stock flags
  • Orders vs Stock shows the coverage gap per SKU with the buffer maths explained
  • Stock exceptions track negative-stock and transfer shortages to resolution
19

Stock Transfers

Movements between warehouses — why one depot is short while the business is not.

  • Filter by date and by lane, with a route summary when a filter is on
  • Filtered views go always-live rather than reading the cache
20

Physical Stock

Every time the books were corrected to what is actually on the shelf.

  • Each row leads with the net adjustment, up or down
  • Counts can be entered in packs or base units
  • This is what keeps net stock honest enough to divide by velocity

Reading the year

Demand history, seasonality and the lines that carry the business.

5 screens
23

Sales Report

Where the units went: how many invoices, to whom, and which designs moved.

  • Indian financial-year presets, plus explicit date ranges
  • Compare against last year, with equal windows stated
  • Breakdown across items, groups, catalogs and customers, drillable by breadcrumb
24

Purchases Report

Real import cadence per factory, against the lead times Purchasing assumes.

  • Invoice and vendor counts with year-on-year deltas
  • Monthly bar chart
  • Top vendors by invoice count
25

Inventory Report

Which products carry the business, and which have quietly stopped.

  • Hero products by units sold, with year-on-year movement
  • Dead stock grouped by urgency — critical over 180 days idle, high 90–180, watch under 90
  • Each row shows quantity, last-moved date, and days idle
26

Finished Goods Sales

Three financial years side by side, by catalog or by design.

  • Each card leads with this year’s units and the year-on-year delta — every chart scales its own axis, so the number is what makes them comparable
  • Period, warehouse and which years to show sit behind one settings control
  • Tap any bar to inspect that month
27

Inside a catalog

One catalog’s year, its designs ranked, and what the table will not tell you.

  • Tap a bar for a per-year readout of that month
  • “Worth knowing” surfaces concentration risk — a group leaning on a single SKU
  • Each design row carries best month, how many of its SKUs are selling, and its seasonal peak

Setup

Getting 1,500 SKUs in, and who is allowed to touch them.

3 screens
21

Import

The Excel round-trip: pull the catalogue out, edit it, push it back.

  • Download current products, or start from a blank file
  • Map your own columns onto Clio’s ten fields; the mapping is remembered
  • Review resolves every row as create or update before a single write, then imports in chunks
22

Team

Who can open Clio and what they can do.

  • Owner, admin, manager, viewer — all money-free
  • Deactivate without removing
  • Per-module permissions behind the member form
28

Settings

Appearance, the business record, and the one purchasing feature flag.

  • Design languages drawn as miniature app mocks — glass is the default
  • Accent colour, font and display sizing
  • Shipping containers on or off reshapes the whole order → container → arrival chain

How this was captured

Source
The live seeded demo — 3 catalogs, 20 SKUs, 4 factories, 41 months of purchase and sale history, so every runway, order-by date and velocity on screen is computed from real movement rather than mocked.
Theme
Clio's glass design language, pinned before first paint via the app's own appearance store and asserted on every capture.
Method
Playwright driving Chrome. Desktop 1440×900 at 2× and phone 390×844 at 3× with a real iOS user-agent and touch enabled, so mobile shots show the floating dock rather than a squeezed desktop. Screens that only exist after an interaction — action sheets, the SKU story, the catalog drill-down — are driven to that state before the shot.
Money
Deliberately absent everywhere, including voucher entry. Quantity is the only currency.
